    How to read this

    Catchment circles show how far from the school the last child was offered a place in the normal admissions round (Reception for primaries, Year 7 for secondaries). London schools mostly have no fixed boundary, so the distance changes every year.

    Faith schools rank baptised, practising families first, so their non-faith distance says little about your chances with a priest's reference.

    Not shown, because they are boys or girls only: .

    Sold prices: HM Land Registry Price Paid Data, standard sales over the last two years in each area's postcode sectors. It does not record bedrooms, so flats (mostly 1–2 bed) and houses (mostly 2–4 bed) are shown separately. The price level compares each neighbourhood (an ONS Census LSOA, about 650 homes) with the shortlist median for the same property type, so 100 is typical and 150 is half as dear again.

    Sweet spots: a neighbourhood passes if its price level, its crime rate and the straight-line distance from its middle to the nearest Good or Outstanding primary and secondary on this map are all within the sliders. Being near a school is not the same as being in its catchment, and a low price level can mean smaller or ex-council homes rather than a bargain, so treat it as a shortlist of streets to look at, not an answer.

    New developments: approved schemes of 20 or more homes from the GLA's Planning London Datahub (London only) that finished since 2023, started since 2020 or were approved since 2022, joined to HM Land Registry new-build sales since 2023 by location; buildings with 5+ new-build sales and no matching scheme are added too, which covers the areas outside London. "Selling" means a new-build sale within 6 months of the newest new-build sale in the Land Registry data, which currently ends in April 2026: new builds are registered months after completion, so recent and off-plan sales are missing. Sales under £250k are counted as probable shared-ownership shares and left out of the price range. Sales are matched from 2020, because flats usually sell before the council records a scheme as finished. "Finished" means no new-build sales were found: a build-to-rent block (planning records often count these as market homes, as at Cherry Park in Stratford), affordable homes, or sales not registered yet for schemes finished in the last year. Student housing, co-living, care and extra-care homes, supported living and temporary accommodation are left out. Planning names are often site addresses or what the land was before ("Lodge Lane Car Park site" is 98 new homes on today's car park): the popup quotes the plan.

    Conservation areas: planning.data.gov.uk (each council's designations, Nov 2026). Inside one, the look of the street is protected, which usually supports prices, but extensions, loft dormers, new windows, cladding and front gardens need more permission, and trees need 6 weeks' notice before work. Some also have an Article 4 direction, which takes away more permitted development: the council's appraisal says. The card's share counts the area's sold homes from the last two years that are inside one, a rough stand-in for the share of homes.

    Crime: police.uk street-level crime for 12 months. The card's 1-mile count is the same radius everywhere, so it is not per person; the rates per 1,000 residents count every crime inside the area's neighbourhoods (the same Census LSOAs as the prices) and divide by their 2021 population. police.uk moves each crime to the nearest of a fixed set of points (usually the middle of a street), so a point means "on or near this street", never an address. Fraud and some sensitive offences are not published. Shoplifting is left out of "all", as it piles onto shopping streets.

    Pupil numbers: DfE Get Information about Schools, SchoolDash and WhatSchool (Nov 2026). Rents and journey times are estimates. Rightmove links open the map view with 3+ bedrooms, houses only, garden, up to £4,500 a month. "This view on Rightmove" (bottom left of the map) searches exactly the part of the map you can see, slightly enlarged, to rent or for sale, with the same filters (no price limit for sale). Ofsted: since Sept 2024 there are no new single-word grades; we show the last one and whether a later short visit kept it, or the new report card.
